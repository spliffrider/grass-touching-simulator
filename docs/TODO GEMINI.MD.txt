This project manual is incredibly clean and well-architected. The strict separation where `GameScene` handles orchestration and visuals while the `src/game/systems/` directory owns the state-mutating gameplay logic is exactly what you want for a scalable incremental game. It's also the perfect structure for a vibe-coding workflow with an agent-first IDE like Antigravity, as you can easily isolate context window updates to just the specific system and data files you are expanding.

To push the simulation aspects and introduce some roguelike elements into Cozy Grass Touching Simulator, here are a few systems you could add to drastically increase the depth, complexity, and decision-making for the player.

### 1. Spatial Synergies (Grid-Based Placement)

Right now, your animals are passive inventory companions and your `SprinklerSystem` acts as a baseline automation. You can add massive simulation depth by making the grid matter.

* **The Concept:** Allow players to place Sprinklers, Bee Hives, or new structures directly onto specific `tileKey(x, y)` coordinates. These structures buff adjacent tiles (e.g., a Bee Hive makes surrounding grass regrow 20% faster; a Sprinkler waters a 3x3 area).


* **Implementation:**
* Create a `PlacementSystem.ts` in `src/game/systems/`.


* Update `AnimalCompanionSystem` and `SprinklerSystem` to query neighbors using your existing `getFieldBounds(state)` and `getFieldTiles(state)` helpers.


* If a player places a Chicken near a `grass-golden.png` tile, maybe it has a chance to dig up premium artifacts.





### 2. A Prestige / Rebirth System (The Roguelike Loop)

Every great incremental game needs a meta-progression loop. Once the player unlocks everything in the `seed-shop.ts` and maxes their `UPGRADES` tree, they need a reason to reset.

* **The Concept:** The player "mows the world," resetting their field, standard currencies, and basic upgrades, but they earn a new persistent meta-currency (e.g., "Golden Clippings" or "Life Force"). This currency is spent on permanent, game-altering mutations.
* **Implementation:**
* Add the new meta-currency and lifetime total to `GameState` in `src/game/types/game-state.ts`.


* Create a `PrestigeSystem.ts` that handles the wipe, utilizing `createInitialState` but retaining the new meta-currency and specific milestone flags.


* Add a new shop data file (e.g., `src/game/data/mutations.ts`) where purchases tap into `UpgradeSystem` to apply massive multipliers to `RuntimeStats`.





### 3. Cross-Breeding & Genetics (Crafting)

Instead of just buying the next grass tier from the `seed-shop.ts`, make the player earn it through experimentation.

* **The Concept:** When a `grass-thick.png` tile and a `grass-clover.png` tile grow next to each other, they have a small chance to spawn a mutated seed drop.


* **Implementation:**
* Create a `MutationSystem.ts` that runs on a timer.
* It scans `getGrownTiles(state)` looking for specific adjacency pairs.


* When a pair is found, it triggers a feedback callback to `GameScene` to pop a visual effect and drops a new seed type into the player's inventory using `InventorySystem.ts` helpers.





### 4. Dynamic Weather Events & Invasions

You already have a `weather_jar` unlocking weather mechanics. You can turn this into an active gameplay layer to balance out the passive automation.

* **The Concept:** Certain weather types (defined in `src/game/data/weather.ts`) bring dynamic challenges. For example, a "Drought" slows regrowth, or a "Windstorm" blows in Invasive Weeds. Weeds must be actively clicked (touched) to remove them, or they will slowly overwrite adjacent grass tiles, ruining spatial synergies.


* **Implementation:**
* Expand `WEATHER_TYPES` to include an `onTick` or `spawnChance` property.


* Create an `InvasionSystem.ts` that updates the `Record<TileKey, FieldTile>` by flipping regular grass to weeds.


* Return `changed: true` so `GameScene` knows to save the game and update the visuals.





### 5. Equipment & Tools

Give the player a loadout.

* **The Concept:** Instead of just passive upgrades, the player can equip specific gloves or boots that change how they interact with the game. A "Heavy Boot" might harvest a 3x3 area but disable critical hits, while "Golden Shears" double gold drops but slow down the base touch rate.
* **Implementation:**
* Add an `equippedGear` property to `GameState`.


* Add a new folder/file like `src/game/data/equipment.ts` for definitions.
* Wire it into the existing `UpgradeSystem` so that equipped gear dynamically modifies `RuntimeStats` during the `GameScene.update` loop.

Because you've kept your `UpgradeSystem` and `RuntimeStats` so cleanly decoupled from the UI, layering any of these mathematical complexities on top will be remarkably smooth.
